I've been running some bits of the playtest of Crows, MCDM's new dungeon
crawling game. Overall I really like it. However, I have struggled with an
element of the world building of Cornath, the built-in setting of Crows. **In
Cornath, magic is sentient, and it does not like to be used**.

> The chaotic, sentient force of magic doesn’t like to be manipulated, and
> lashes out against those who seek to control it when their grasp of a spell is
> weak.

This certainly feels right for the setting. It's dark and post-apocalyptic, and
the unknowing mistreatment of sentient magic is pretty key to the lore of that
apocalypse. It makes sense that the world is fighting you.

However, I feel this non-trivially restricts the kinds of magic users you can
play in this world. Either your character doesn't know about this property of
magic and is unknowingly forcing a sentient being to do things against their
will, or your character **does** know about this and has decided that it is
worth it.

<!--more-->

Now, I understand that this isn't hugely notable. If an adventurer eats meat or
wears leather, they are already benefiting from forcing sentient creatures to do
things against their will. Given the aforementioned dark setting moral greyness
around using magic shouldn't be unexpected, but I think there are several
reasons to be drawn to a morally optimistic take on Crows.

Crows has taken a classic dungeon crawling game loop that focuses on the
acquisition of wealth and made it altruistic. Crows is a rogue-lite game where
the through line is players characters' village, not the player characters
themselves. Hauling back treasure isn't just about personal wealth, it's for the
good of a community.

Additionally, I feel much better about killing Crows monsters than I do many
D&D "monsters". I've historically avoided traditional dungeon crawls because
they usually feel like invading the home of some Goblins or Kobalds (which are
just people) and then killing them and taking their stuff. That is not how Crows
feels. Crows feels like you are fighting against non-sapient (though admittedly
still sentient) forces of nature to try to recover resources to survive the
aftermath of a natural disaster.

The obvious way to address my complaint is to lean in. Crows is not being
inconsistent. It isn't actually an optimistic game. It's a survival game.
Maybe to survive you need to make hard choices and, ultimately, a game is a
series of interesting choices. The implications of using magic adds more nuance
to the decision to cast a spell. Is causing this harm worth it for my survival?
Is this worth it from my _community's_ survival?

The easiest way to address my complaint is to ignore it and, honestly, that's
what I've done. If I mentioned magic being sentient to my players it was only in
passing in the first session. We haven't focused on it, and my players just cast
spells like they would in any other game or setting. This works perfectly fine,
but I do wonder if I've lost something.

Two more approaches came up when I raised this in the Crows channel in MCDM's
discord. I'm not sure if the person who raised them would want to be mentioned
here, but if you join the MCDM discord you can find the relevant messages
[here](https://discord.com/channels/332362513368875008/1508499407996321955/1557268969985933374)
and
[here](https://discord.com/channels/332362513368875008/1508499407996321955/1557270734345211955).

One approach is just to acknowledge that this is a fantasy world. There can be
objective evil. Maybe magic is fundamentally, objectively, evil. Maybe everyone
agrees it is okay to harm it because if given the chance it would destroy all
order and life and leave only chaos, or something like that.

The other approach is that **magic is a wolf**. It is sapient, it does not like
to be used, and it has bitten people who have tried to use it before. But that
doesn't mean that _you_ can't befriend it, that _you_ can't work with it.

All of which is to say that I still feel weird about sentient magic, but it
certainly isn't ruining the game for me, and I can imagine ways that it could
enrich it.
