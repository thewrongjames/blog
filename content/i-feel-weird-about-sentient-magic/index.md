I've been running some bits of the playtest of Crows, MCDMs new dungeon crawling
game. Overall I really like it. I'm trying to play more games that have a
lighter focus on making building characters fun and a heavier focus on making
playing characters fun. There are certainly games that fit that description
better than Crows (and I'm also trying Mothership and Mythic Bastionland), but
I'm coming from mainly playing D&D 5e and Pathfinder 2e, so this is a step in
the right direction.

However, I have struggled with an element of the world building of Cornath, the
built-in setting of Crows. **In Cornath, magic is sentient, and it does not like
to be used**.

<!--more-->

> The chaotic, sentient force of magic doesn’t like to be manipulated, and
> lashes out against those who seek to control it when their grasp of a spell is
> weak.

This certainly feels right for the setting. It's dark and post-apocalyptic, and
magic being sentient is pretty key to the lore of that apocalypse. It makes
sense that you can't get something for free and that the world is fighting you.

However, I feel like this non-trivially restricts the kinds of magic users that
you can play in this world. Either your character doesn't know about this
property of magic and is unknowingly forcing a sentient being to do things
against their will, or your character **does** know about this and has decided
that it is worth it.

Now, I understand that this isn't hugely notable. If an adventurer eats meat or
wears leather, they are already benefiting from forcing sentient creatures to do
things against their will. Still, I think it is notable that I feel a style of
play in which I cast spells without wondering whether it is intrinsically cruel
has been closed off to me.

Given the aforementioned dark and post-apocalyptic setting perhaps I shouldn't
expect this game to allow me such a style of play, but I think there are several
reasons to be drawn to a morally optimistic take on Crows.

Crows has taken a classic dungeon crawling game loop that focuses on the
acquisition of wealth and made it altruistic. Crows is built like a rogue-lite
game where the through line is players characters' village, not the player
characters themselves. Hauling back treasure isn't just about personal wealth,
it's for the good of a community.

Additionally, I feel much better about killing Crows monsters than I do many
D&D monsters. I've historically avoided traditional dungeon crawls because they
usually feel like invading the home of some Goblins of Kobalds (which are just
people), killing them, and taking their stuff. That is not how Crows feels.
Crows feels like you are trying to recover resources after a natural disaster
and are fighting against non-sapient (though admittedly still sentient) forces
of nature.

The most obvious way to address my complain here is to not address it. Crows is
not being inconsistent. It isn't actually an optimistic game. It's a survival
game. Sometimes to survive you need to make hard choices. And, ultimately, a
game is a series of interesting decisions. The implications of using magic adds
a trade off to every choice. Is this worth it for my survival? Is this worth it
from my _community's_ survival?

Lean in.

Ignore it.

It's a wolf.
